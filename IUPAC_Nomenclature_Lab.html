What is IUPAC Nomenclature?

A single systematic way to name every organic compound, so a chemist anywhere in the world understands the same structure from the same name.

IUPAC (International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry) nomenclature gives every organic molecule one correct, unambiguous name built from its carbon skeleton, its functional group, and the substituents attached to it.

  • ?Without it, the same molecule could be called by dozens of local or trade names — IUPAC names remove that confusion.
  • ⚙Used in textbooks, research papers, drug labels, industry, and every chemistry examination.
  • 🎯In this lab you will learn to choose the parent chain, number it correctly, name substituents, and assemble the final IUPAC name — by building molecules yourself.

Quick Reference

Order of precedence of functional groups (highest first), used to choose the parent chain, the principal suffix, and the numbering direction.

Functional Group Suffix Example
IUPAC names are written as a single word (except salts, acids and derivatives). Commas separate adjacent numbers; hyphens separate numbers from letters. Example: 2,2-Dimethyl-3-hexene.
Italicised structural prefixes such as sec-, tert-, cis-, trans- are joined by a hyphen and are ignored when alphabetising. Multiplying prefixes di-, tri-, tetra- are part of the name but are also ignored when alphabetising substituents.
